The brief

For this project I had to design my own fried rice, cook it twice in class, and then serve it to a real person and see what they thought.

My user: Mr Omar Negm

I asked Mr Omar, a teacher at FDR, if he could taste my dish. He answered by email on September 3:

"I don't have any dietary restrictions. I prefer mild levels of spice. I can be available during lunch time on any day. Just let me know a few days in advance."

MildHe doesn't like spicy food, so I didn't use any chili or ají. The only thing close to spice is black pepper.
LunchHe would eat it at lunch, so it had to be a real meal with rice, chicken and egg, not just a taste.
NoticeHe asked for a few days' notice, so I set the date with him by email ahead of time.

Research

Almost every country has its own fried rice. I looked at three of them to see how they are made and what I could use in mine.

Peruarroz chaufa

Chaufa

Chinese workers started arriving in Peru in 1849 and their cooking mixed with Peruvian food, which became chifa. Chaufa uses sillao, cebolla china and egg, and it is cooked in a very hot wok in a few minutes.

What I tookCooking on high heat, and using sillao and cebolla china.
Philippinessinangag

Garlic fried rice

A breakfast rice made with leftover rice and a lot of garlic, usually served with a fried egg and meat. It is made with old rice on purpose, because fresh rice sticks together.

What I tookUsing rice from the day before, and garlic as the base.
Thailandkhao pad sapparot

Pineapple fried rice

Fried rice with pineapple, cashews and a little curry powder, often served inside half a pineapple. It mixes salty and sweet, and the way it is served makes it look special.

What I tookBalancing the salt with something a bit sweet, like the carrot.

I chose yakimeshi

It got the highest total. It has no chili, all the ingredients are easy to buy in Lima, and I can cook it in one pan in under 20 minutes. It is also a flavor most people here already know from chifa. Sinangag+ had too many extra steps, and I had never made an omelette wrap, so the omurice was a risk.

My dish

"Yakimeshi" means fried rice in Japanese. It came to Peru with Japanese immigrants, who started arriving in 1899, and today it is on most chifa and Nikkei menus.

My yakimeshi in the FDR food lab
RiceWhite rice cooked the day before, so it is drier and the grains don't stick
ProteinChicken breast in small cubes and 2 eggs, scrambled soft
VegetablesCarrot cut very small, and cebolla china (some cooked, some fresh on top)
FlavorGarlic, sillao, sesame oil, salt and pepper. No chili
My twistSesame oil added at the end so it keeps its smell, and rice left a bit toasted
CultureJapanese dish, made the way it is in Peruvian chifas

Tip I learned

Use a little sillao and a lot of heat. The rice should come out loose and a bit toasted. If you add too much sillao at once, it gets wet and sticky.

I cooked the rice the night before and kept it in the fridge.

Food safety

We cooked with hot oil on induction plates, and I was working with raw chicken, so I followed these rules.

Cutting order

  1. Cebolla china
  2. Carrot
  3. Garlic
  4. Chicken last, because raw chicken can spread bacteria. After cutting it I washed the knife and board and changed gloves.

Gloves and mask on the whole time.

Equipment

  • Wok or large pan and an induction plate
  • Spatula, knife and cutting board
  • Bowl for the beaten eggs
  • Thermometer to check the chicken

Oil and water don't mix: I dried everything before it went into the hot pan.

Temperatures

74 °Cinside the chicken
  • Chicken has to reach 74 °C in the middle to be safe
  • Cooked rice can grow bacteria if it stays warm, so it goes in the fridge within 1 hour and is used within a day
  • Plates unplugged before serving

Prototypes 1 & 2

I cooked it twice in the FDR food lab. We worked in pairs, but each of us cooked our own dish.

Cutting the carrot while the chicken cooks
The rice with chicken, egg and carrot

What went well

  • The chicken got golden and stayed juicy
  • The day-old rice didn't turn into mush
  • The carrot kept its color and crunch

What I changed

  • I used less sillao and added it slowly, so the rice didn't get wet
  • I turned up the heat and stirred less, so the rice toasted a bit
  • I took the eggs out earlier so they stayed soft

Testing it with Mr Omar

Mr Omar tried the final version at lunch. Then I compared the dish with what he asked for in the brief.

What he asked forDid I meet it?
Mild spiceYes. No chili, only black pepper
Something for lunchYes. Rice, chicken and egg in one plate
A few days' noticeYes. We set the date by email
An original dishPartly. It is a known dish, but the balance of sillao and sesame is mine
Good platingNot yet. I served it straight from the pan
If I made it again

I would press the rice into a bowl and turn it over onto the plate so it looks neater, with fresh cebolla china on top. I would also leave the rice a little longer on the heat so it gets more toasted, and maybe add the crispy garlic chips from my Sinangag+ idea for more crunch.
