The problem

In this unit we had to design our own fried rice, not follow a recipe. The dish had to be made for a real person and cooked in class.

I need to design a fried rice that a real user will enjoy at lunch. It has to be mild, filling and safe, and I have to be able to cook it by myself in one class.My problem statement

Why it is a real problem

Fried rice looks easy, but it goes wrong a lot. If the rice is fresh it gets sticky. If you add too much soy sauce it gets wet. If the pan is not hot enough the rice steams instead of frying. On top of that, a lot of fried rice in Lima is made with ají or hot sauce, and my user doesn't like spicy food.

A lot of fried rice is made spicy · my user wants it mild

Limits from the lab

  • 60 minutes to set up, cook, plate and clean
  • We work in pairs, but each student cooks their own dish
  • I bring all the ingredients and pre-cook what needs it
  • Induction plates and one pan or griddle each
  • Two prototypes, then the final dish is tested with the user
  • Hot oil and water are a danger, so equipment is checked and unplugged

Secondary research

I read about where fried rice comes from and the science of why some rice fries better than others.

How fried rice got to Peru

1849

Chinese workers start arriving in Peru. Their cooking mixes with Peruvian ingredients and becomes chifa. Arroz chaufa is its most famous dish.

1899

The ship Sakura Maru arrives in Callao with 790 Japanese immigrants. Japanese food later mixes with Peruvian food too, which is called Nikkei cuisine.

"Yakimeshi" is Japanese for fried rice. In Lima you find it in Nikkei restaurants and many chifas.

Why day-old rice works

When cooked rice is chilled, the starch inside the grains crystallizes. The rice gets drier and harder, so the grains separate instead of sticking together in the pan.

Rice can be dangerous

Cooked rice can grow bacteria (Bacillus cereus) if it is left warm. The UK Food Standards Agency says to cool it within 1 hour, keep it in the fridge and use it within a day.

What the experts say

I looked for advice from people who cook and teach for a living.

"The starch molecules in cooked rice crystallize during chilling, and the hard, dry clumps separate easily into individual grains."Lan Lam · test cook, Cook's Illustrated
For fluffy scrambled eggs, pour them into oil "that's just beginning to smoke" so the water turns to steam and they puff up.Lan Lam · Cook's Illustrated

What I took from this

Use rice from the day before, get the pan very hot before the egg goes in, and keep the seasoning simple so the rice is the main thing.

"Safety first: hot oil + water = danger. Check and unplug equipment. Bring all ingredients and pre-cook ingredients that need it."Mr Velasquez · Food Design teacher
"Keep updating your Food Design Report with evidence, feedback, and improvements. Then test your Final Solution with your real user and use their feedback!"Mr Velasquez · Classroom, Sep 9

What I took from this

Plan the order of cooking, dry everything before it touches hot oil, and take photos every time I cook so I have evidence.

Idea 1: Chicken Yakimeshi

Safe / fusion route
Reference · yakimeshi

What it is

Japanese-style fried rice the way it is made in Lima's chifas and Nikkei restaurants. Day-old rice fried on high heat with chicken, soft scrambled egg, carrot and cebolla china, seasoned with sillao and a little sesame oil.

Why I thought of it

It is a dish people in Lima already know and like, so it is a safe choice for my user. It has no chili at all. It only needs one pan, and every ingredient is easy to buy. It also connects two cultures that are part of Peru: Japanese and chifa.

Cooking plan: the 60 minutes

I planned the lab minute by minute so I would not run out of time.

0–10 minWash hands, gloves and mask on. Set up the plate and pans. Cut cebolla china, carrot and garlic.
10–15 minCut the chicken last on its own board. Wash the knife and board, change gloves.
15–22 minBrown the chicken on high heat with salt and pepper. Take it out.
22–25 minScramble the eggs soft. Take them out. Sauté garlic, carrot and half the cebolla china.
25–32 minAdd the cold rice, break it up, fry 3–4 min. Chicken and egg back in. Sillao little by little, sesame oil, rest of the cebolla china.
32–60 minPlate and take photos. Taste and get feedback. Unplug, wash and clean the station.

Tip I followed

Use a little sillao and a lot of heat. The rice should come out loose and a bit toasted, not wet. If you pour all the sillao at once, it gets soggy.

Safety rules

  • Chicken cut last and cooked to 74 °C inside
  • Nothing wet goes into hot oil
  • Rice stays in the fridge until I cook it
  • Plates unplugged before I leave the station

Prototype 1

My first time cooking the yakimeshi in the FDR food lab. My partner cooked his own dish next to me.

Prototype 1 · cutting carrot while the chicken cooks

What I did

I diced the carrot very small and sautéed it in a separate pot. I put the chicken breast in the pan whole, planning to cut it after it was cooked. Then I fried the day-old rice and mixed everything with the egg and sillao.

What I noticed

  • Cooking the breast whole was slow and the outside browned before the inside was done
  • Using two pans made it harder to control
  • The day-old rice didn't turn into mush

Final cooking

For the final dish I cooked the prototype 2 recipe again, fresh, and served it to Mr Omar at lunch.

What the final recipe kept from prototype 2

  • Chicken breast cut into small cubes before cooking
  • Everything cooked on one big griddle, so the rice fries and doesn't steam

  • Eggs scrambled soft and mixed in at the end
  • Sesame oil and fresh cebolla china to finish

How I tested my dish

I used different methods so I didn't depend only on my own opinion.

User taste test

Mr Omar tasted the final dish at lunch and told me what he thought. This tells me if it works for the real user.

Specs checklist

After every prototype I checked the dish against my 9 design specifications, marking Yes, Partly or No. This let me compare prototype 1 and 2 fairly.

Comparing in the lab

My partner cooked his own dish next to me, so I could compare how we did the same steps and what came out better.

Photos

I took photos while cooking so I could look at the rice texture and colors after, and to have evidence.

Timing

I checked how long each step took against my 60-minute plan to see if the dish was realistic for the lab.

Safety check

I checked that the chicken was cooked all the way through (74 °C) before serving it to anyone.

What I would improve

If I had to cook it again, these are the changes I would make.

Make it more original

Yakimeshi is a known dish. I would add my own twist, like the crispy garlic chips from my Sinangag+ idea, to give it more crunch and make it more mine.

Better texture

Some rice still clumped. I would break it up with my hands before cooking and leave it a bit longer on the heat without stirring so it toasts more.

Plating

Serve it in a molded dome with cebolla china and maybe a few sesame seeds on top, on a white plate so the colors stand out.

Measure portions

Weigh each plate so both portions are the same and I know it is really around 350 to 400 g.

Impact of my solution

On my user

Mr Omar got a lunch made around what he actually likes: mild, filling and hot. It shows that fried rice doesn't need to be spicy to have flavor.

Less food waste

Fried rice is made with rice from the day before. A lot of leftover rice gets thrown away at home, and this dish turns it into a full meal.

Health

It has lean protein from chicken breast and egg, vegetables, and only 1 tablespoon of oil for two plates.

Culture and me

It shows how Chinese and Japanese immigrants changed the food of Peru. For me, I learned to plan a cook, work safely with raw chicken, and use feedback to improve.
